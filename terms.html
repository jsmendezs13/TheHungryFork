Checking in at a restaurant

A check-in is a record that you were at a restaurant on a particular day. You tap Check In, your phone shows a code for ninety seconds, and a member of restaurant staff confirms it. When you arrive with a booking and staff scan the booking’s QR code at the door, that counts as a check-in too, for the account that made the booking or the account with the booking’s phone number. Checking in is optional; it is what makes your visit count for a restaurant’s rewards — see Rewards, Lucky Bite and invites below.

  • One check-in per restaurant per day. Showing the code again the same day adds nothing.
  • Only a member of that restaurant’s staff can confirm a visit, and never their own.
  • Check in only when you are really there. Do not ask staff to confirm a visit that did not happen, and do not lend your code to somebody else — it would record the visit against your account anyway.
  • We can remove check-ins we have good reason to believe are not real, and suspend an account that does this repeatedly.

A check-in is a record, not a promise. It does not by itself entitle you to anything. Where a restaurant chooses to offer something for returning customers, that offer is the restaurant’s — see Rewards, Lucky Bite and invites below.

Fork’s Rating does not check each decision: we rely in good faith on restaurants to follow these rules.

It may reply to your review in public, and you may answer.

Menus, prices and allergens

Restaurant staff maintain their own menus here: prices, descriptions, what is sold out. We do not verify what they enter, and it can be out of date or wrong.

If you have a food allergy or intolerance, do not rely on this website. Allergen information shown here is provided by the restaurant, may be incomplete, and can change without notice — recipes and suppliers change, and kitchens share equipment.

Always speak to restaurant staff directly before you order. Seb’s Analytics LLC does not prepare the food and is not responsible for what a kitchen serves you.

Sanitary inspection grades

In New York City, the Health Department inspects restaurants and gives each one a letter grade — A, B or C — on a card the restaurant must post where people passing by can see it.

The grade shown on a restaurant’s pages here is chosen by that restaurant. A restaurant can show A, B or C, or choose to show no grade at all: whether to show it here is up to the restaurant. Seb’s Analytics LLC and Fork’s Rating do not inspect restaurants, and we do not check, confirm or update the grade a restaurant shows. The restaurant is responsible for showing only the grade on its current Health Department card, and for changing or removing it when that grade changes.

The official grade is the card posted at the restaurant and the inspection results the NYC Health Department publishes on nyc.gov. If what you see here is different, the Health Department’s grade is the right one — please tell us at advisory@sebsanalytics.com.

How each one works, where a restaurant runs it:

  • Lucky Bite.
