Review rules

The Hungry Fork · Last updated 10 November 2026

Reviews on Fork’s Rating come from guests with a verified US phone number. Each restaurant manages the reviews on its own website, following these rules.

Every review is published right away. No restaurant can hold a review back, delete it or change it.

These rules are based on the U.S. Federal Trade Commission (FTC) rule on consumer reviews, 16 CFR 465.7 (read it on the FTC’s website). Under them, a restaurant can hide a review only for one of these reasons.

Named in the FTC rule

  1. Confidential business information
  2. Defamatory, harassing, abusive, obscene, vulgar or sexual content
  3. Someone’s personal information
  4. Discrimination (race, gender, sexuality, ethnicity or a similar trait)
  5. Clearly false or misleading information
  6. A fake review
  7. Something not about the restaurant or its food

Other neutral reasons

Used the same way for a 5-star and a 1-star review:

  1. Demands or threats from the writer (for example, “a free dinner or 1 star”)
  2. Ads, sales links or contact details, and scam links
  3. Pretending to be someone else
  4. A conflict of interest (a competitor, a supplier, or a relative or close friend of the owner or staff), when the restaurant knows it for sure
  5. Meaningless text
  6. Illegal or dangerous content
  7. Not the writer’s own experience, when the review says so
  8. The same review posted twice
  9. Another reason, which the restaurant explains

A restaurant can never hide a review because it is negative or has a low score, or because it complains about the food, the service, the staff, the waiting time, a booking, the prices or the cleanliness.

A hidden review is hidden whole (comment and stars) and doesn’t count in the rating. The same rules apply to every review, whatever its stars. The author can still see it, and the restaurant tells them why.

Reviews by the restaurant’s team: ratings by its owners and managers are hidden automatically, and its crew can’t rate it while they work there.

The restaurants on Fork’s Rating are clients of Seb’s Analytics LLC, which builds their websites. No restaurant pays to change its score.

Nobody can change a review once it is posted, not even its author. Rating a dish again (30 days later) adds a new review, and every rating counts. Only the author can delete their review.

Your review was hidden? Write to the restaurant from My Tastings (Message the restaurant, under your review); it can show your review again. Still think it’s a mistake, or something else is wrong? Write to Fork’s Rating at advisory@sebsanalytics.com.
