Privacy Policy

The Hungry Fork · Last updated 16 September 2026

The short version. We ask for your name, phone number, date of birth and gender so you can have an account and rate dishes. The name you give is shown publicly on the reviews you write. Your phone number is never shown to anyone. We never sell your personal information.

If you choose to check in at a restaurant, we also keep a record of that visit. We never track where your phone is.

The rest of this page explains exactly what we hold, why, and who else touches it.

The Hungry Fork is operated by Seb’s Analytics LLC, a limited liability company in the State of New York. This policy covers thehungryfork.fun.

Cookies and what stays on your phone

We do not use cookies or tracking pixels. We do store two small pieces of information in your own browser, on your own device: the fact that you are logged in, and any dishes you have saved as favourites. They never leave your device, and clearing your browser data removes them.

Checking in at a restaurant

If you want a record of the restaurants you have been to, you can check in. It works like this:

  • You tap Check In and your phone shows a code for 90 seconds.
  • A member of restaurant staff scans it and confirms you are there.
  • We record the restaurant, the date, and which member of staff confirmed it.

Three things worth knowing:

  • The code holds nothing about you. Not your name, not your number, not your account. If somebody photographs it over your shoulder, they have a ticket that stops working in a minute and a half — and using it would only record a visit for you.
  • Only one visit per restaurant per day is recorded, whatever happens.
  • It is entirely your choice. You can eat at every restaurant on this site and never check in once. Nothing else on The Hungry Fork depends on it.

Research

Seb’s Analytics LLC studies how people choose food — how prices, dishes, age and gender relate to what people enjoy. Your ratings feed that research, and so does how often people return to a restaurant, if you check in.

Two commitments about it:

  • Only group figures ever leave. Anything we publish, present or share with a client is a statistic about a group — an average, a percentage, a count. Never a row about a person.
  • We never publish anything that identifies you. Your name, phone number, date of birth and gender are never attached to anything we share, in any form.

Your reviews remain public on this site under your name and handle, as they are today. That is separate from the research and is how a review site works.

How long we keep it

We keep your account information for as long as your account exists. If you ask us to delete your account, we delete your name, phone number, date of birth and gender.

Reviews are handled separately: a review can be detached from your account rather than erased, so that a restaurant’s rating does not change because someone left. If you want your reviews removed as well, say so and we will remove them.

Your check-ins are deleted with your account — all of them, in full. Unlike a review, nobody else’s rating depends on them. You can also ask us to delete your visit history and keep the rest of your account.

Your choices

  • See what we hold about you. Ask, and we will send it.
  • Correct it. Ask, and we will fix it.
  • Delete it. Ask, and we will delete your account as described above.
  • Stop hearing about promotions at any time.

Write to advisory@sebsanalytics.com from a message we can connect to your account, or tell us the phone number on the account. We reply within 30 days.

Age

You must be at least 13 years old to create an account. We do not knowingly collect information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has an account, write to advisory@sebsanalytics.com and we will delete it.

Keeping it safe

Your PIN is stored scrambled, protected by a secret key held outside the database, so that even someone who obtained a copy of the database could not work out your PIN. Access to customer information is restricted to the server itself; the code that runs in your browser cannot read it. Nothing online is ever perfectly safe, and we do not claim otherwise — but if there is ever a breach affecting your information, we will tell you.

Changes

If we change this policy, the new version appears here with a new date at the top. If a change materially affects how we use your information, we will make it clear rather than hoping you re-read the page.
